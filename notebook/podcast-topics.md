# Podcast topics

A bank of things to talk about with your three friends, written for senior year of college. Each topic has a few starter questions. You don't need all of them: pick one or two topics per friend, or one topic for the whole episode and ask all three friends the same questions so the segments can be compared (the [episode plan](../research/outputs/05-episode-and-interviews.md) explains why that helps).

Questions marked ⭐ tend to get the best stories, because they ask for a specific moment instead of an opinion.

## Dating and relationships

- ⭐ What's the most "only in college" dating story you have?
- Is dating apps or meeting people in real life working better for you right now? Why?
- What's a green flag you didn't used to care about, and a red flag you used to ignore?
- Situationships: are they a phase, or the new normal?
- Does senior year make dating feel more serious, or less? Is anyone starting something they know might end at graduation?
- How do you date when everyone's about to move to different cities?
- ⭐ What did your first relationship teach you that you still use?
- What advice would you give a first-year about dating here?

## Friendship

- ⭐ How did we actually become friends? (Each of you tells it, then compare versions.)
- Which friendships from first year are still here, and what made them last?
- Is it harder to make new friends senior year, or easier because no one's pretending anymore?
- What's something a friend did for you that you never properly thanked them for?
- How do you stay close to people after you graduate? Who do you know who's done it well?
- Group chats: what's the dynamic in yours, and who's the one who always replies?

## Real life: becoming an adult

- What's an "adult" skill you only learned recently (taxes, cooking, calling a doctor's office)?
- ⭐ When was the first time you felt like a real adult? When was the last time you definitely didn't?
- Money: what do you actually spend on, and what are you embarrassed to spend on?
- What's your relationship with your family like now compared with when you left for college?
- How do you take care of yourself when things get busy? What actually works and what's just aesthetic?
- What's a habit you're trying to build before you graduate?

## After graduation

- Do you know what you're doing next year? How do you feel when people ask?
- Job, grad school, gap year, moving home: what's the plan, and what's the backup?
- ⭐ What's a path you thought you'd take freshman year that you've completely let go of?
- Are you choosing a city or a job first?
- What are you most scared of about leaving? What are you most excited about?
- What does "success" look like at 25, honestly?

## Advice

- ⭐ What would you tell yourself on move-in day?
- What's the best advice someone gave you here, and who gave it?
- What's some popular advice you think is wrong?
- What do you wish you'd done more of? Less of?
- Advice for someone who feels behind everyone else.
- What's one class, place, or person every student here should find?

## College, looking back

- ⭐ What's the night or day you'll remember most from these four years?
- Which version of you were you in each year? Give each year a title.
- What's the biggest way this place changed you?
- What's something you'll miss that sounds boring (a dining hall, a walk, a study spot)?
- Is there anything you regret not trying?
- What does this location mean to you? (Ties in with filming each friend somewhere that matters to them.)

## Identity and growing up

- What's something you believed at 18 that you've changed your mind about?
- How has your sense of home changed?
- What part of yourself did you find in college that you didn't know was there?
- What's something you're still figuring out and are OK not having figured out?

## Light and fun (good openers, bumpers, or lightning rounds)

- Hot takes: overrated and underrated things on campus.
- Rank the four years, best to worst.
- Your college experience as a movie: title, genre, and who plays you?
- Your most-played song of each year.
- Most chaotic thing in your camera roll right now (with permission to show it on screen).
- Finish the sentence: "Senior year is…"
- A rapid-fire round where all three friends answer the same five questions, cut together in the edit.

## Topics that need care

Some of these get personal fast. Before recording, ask each friend which topics are off-limits, and tell them they can ask you to cut anything afterward. Be extra careful with:

- **Exes and other people's stories.** Anyone named on the podcast hasn't agreed to be on it. Use no names, or change details.
- **Mental health, family, and money.** Good conversations, but let your friend set how deep it goes.
- **Anything about other students or the school** that could follow your friend into a job search. Remember it'll be online.

## Picking a direction

A few ways to turn this list into an episode:

1. **One theme, three angles.** Pick one section (say, *After graduation*) and ask all three friends the same core questions. Easy to edit and compare.
2. **One theme per friend.** Friend 1 talks dating, friend 2 talks friendship, friend 3 talks life after college. Each location can match its topic.
3. **The senior-year time capsule.** Mix the ⭐ questions from every section and frame the episode as a record of who you all are right now, to listen back to in five years.
