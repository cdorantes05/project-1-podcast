# Production, Gear and Distribution: A Beginner's Guide for Carolyn

*Prices are approximate US street prices and change often, so check them before you buy. Sources are linked inline and collected at the end.*

---

## TL;DR

1. **Start with $0.** Use your Mac and earbuds, record in a closet full of clothes, and clean the audio with **Adobe Podcast Enhance**, which is free. As a Harvard College student you also very likely get **Adobe Creative Cloud for free** (Audition and Premiere Pro included).
2. **When you're ready to spend about $100,** get a **USB/XLR dynamic mic** (Samson Q2U or ATR2100x, about $60–80) and closed-back headphones (ATH-M40x, about $80). A dynamic mic matters more than any other purchase in a dorm room.
3. **Record remote guests on Riverside** (the free tier works for testing; Pro costs about $24/mo billed annually) or on **Zencastr** (free plan).
4. **Edit in GarageBand or Audacity** (both free), or use **Descript** if you'd rather edit by editing the transcript. Use **DaVinci Resolve** (free) or Premiere for video.
5. **Master to -16 LUFS for stereo (-19 for mono), with true peak at -1 dBTP.** Export MP3 at 128 kbps (or AAC).
6. **Host for free on Spotify for Creators or RSS.com.** Then submit your RSS feed to Apple Podcasts, and to YouTube through YouTube Studio's "Submit RSS feed."
7. **Massachusetts is an all-party-consent state.** Always tell guests they're being recorded, and have them sign a short release.

## 1. Gear tiers

### USB vs XLR

| | USB | XLR |
|---|---|---|
| How it connects | Plugs straight into the Mac | Needs an audio interface (about $100+) between the mic and the Mac |
| Pros | Cheap, simple, portable | Upgradable, more professional, easier to run two mics |
| Cons | Hard to combine several USB mics on one computer | More money and more cables |
| **Best for Carolyn** | **A hybrid USB+XLR mic.** Start on USB now and move to an interface later without buying a new mic. | |

**Dynamic or condenser?** Choose a **dynamic** mic. Dynamics pick up much less room echo and background noise than condensers, and that's exactly what an untreated dorm room needs ([Unilink 2026](https://www.unilink.us/blog/best-podcast-microphones-2026), [Descript](https://www.descript.com/blog/article/how-to-choose-a-mic-for-your-podcast)). Many cheap "podcast" USB mics, like the Blue Yeti, are condensers. They will capture your roommate, the radiator and the hallway.

### Tier 0: $0 (laptop or phone)

- **Mic:** the built-in MacBook mics are decent at close range. Wired Apple EarPods are often better because the mic sits close to your mouth. iPhone Voice Memos is fine for solo episodes.
- **Headphones:** any wired earbuds.
- **Fix it in software:** run the recordings through [Adobe Podcast Enhance Speech](https://podcast.adobe.com/enhance). The free plan covers about 1 hour a day, with files up to 30 minutes ([ToolRadar](https://toolradar.com/tools/adobe-podcast)).
- **Free campus option:** the **Cabot Media Studios** (Cabot Science Library) are self-service studios with mics, cameras and lighting. The **Lamont Multimedia Lab** lends equipment and runs podcasting workshops ([Harvard Library – Cabot](https://library.harvard.edu/services-tools/cabot-media-studios), [Lamont Multimedia Lab guide](https://guides.library.harvard.edu/multimedia_production/overview)). This may be the best free upgrade available to you.

### Tier 1: about $100

|---|---|---|
| Mic (USB+XLR dynamic) | **Samson Q2U** *or* **Audio-Technica ATR2100x-USB** *or* Fifine K688 | $60 / $60–80 / about $70 |

*If you only have $100,* buy the mic now and use earbuds you already own. Add headphones later.

### Tier 2: about $300

| Item | Pick | Approx. price |
|---|---|---|
| Mic | **Shure MV7+** (USB-C and XLR, built-in processing and auto-gain) | about $250 |
| Headphones | **Sony MDR-7506** (a broadcast standard) | about $100–110 |

### For video

Start with an **iPhone on a $20 tripod**, or your Mac webcam. Light your face with a window in front of you, or a $25–40 LED panel. Record audio on the mic, not the phone. Riverside and Zencastr record local video for remote guests.

## 2. Recording room: cheap dorm fixes

The goal is to **absorb reflections** (echo). Full soundproofing isn't realistic in a dorm.

1. **Record in or facing an open closet full of clothes.** It works as a natural vocal booth ([Fifine](https://fifinemicrophone.com/blogs/news/how-to-make-an-acoustic-treatment-on-a-budget)).
2. **Use soft surfaces.** Put a comforter over your head and mic (the "blanket fort"), hang moving blankets on a curtain rod, or tape an egg-crate mattress topper behind the monitor ([Fifine](https://fifinemicrophone.com/blogs/news/how-to-make-an-acoustic-treatment-on-a-budget), [Making A Scene](https://www.makingascene.org/?p=127900)).
3. **Don't face a bare wall up close.** Sit in the room and point the mic away from hard surfaces.
4. **Keep the mic 2–4 inches (about a fist) from your mouth, slightly off-axis.** Speaking close to a dynamic mic is free noise reduction.
5. **Control the noise sources.** Turn off fans and the AC, close the window, put your phone on Do Not Disturb, and record when the hallway is quiet. Rugs and a made bed help too.
6. **Run Adobe Podcast's free "Mic Check"** to test your setup before you record ([ToolRadar](https://toolradar.com/tools/adobe-podcast)).

### AI helpers

- **Cleanup:** Adobe Podcast Enhance, Descript Studio Sound, and Riverside's audio enhancement.
- **Transcripts:** Descript, Riverside, Premiere's auto-transcribe, and Spotify for Creators' auto-transcripts.
- **Show notes and titles:** Riverside's AI show notes, or paste the transcript into Claude or ChatGPT and fact-check the result.
- **Clips for social:** Riverside Magic Clips (available even on the free tier), Descript's clip tools, and Opus Clip.

Always listen to AI-cleaned audio before you publish. Heavy enhancement can make voices sound robotic.

## 5. Audio standards

| Spec | Target |
|---|---|
| Loudness | **-16 LUFS integrated for stereo, -19 LUFS for mono** ([Podnews](https://podnews.net/article/lufs-lkfs-for-podcasters), [Violet Recording](https://violetrecording.com/podcast-loudness-lufs-standards/)) |
| True peak | **-1 dBTP or lower** (Apple). Spotify's ad spec uses -2 dBTP, so -2 is the safe choice ([Apple](https://podcasters.apple.com/support/audio-requirements), [Spotify ad specs](https://ads.spotify.com/en-GB/ad-specs/podcast-ad-specs/)) |
| Recording format | WAV, 48 kHz (or 44.1), 24-bit. Keep these as your masters. |
| Delivery format | **MP3, 128 kbps stereo (or 64–96 kbps mono), 44.1 kHz.** Apple prefers AAC (.m4a) at 128–256 kbps stereo or 64–128 kbps mono ([Apple](https://podcasters.apple.com/support/audio-requirements), [Castos](https://support.castos.com/article/177-audio-encoding-guidance)) |
| Video | 1080p MP4 (H.264) for YouTube and Spotify |

**How to hit -16 LUFS:** use Audacity's Loudness Normalization, Audition's Match Loudness, Descript's export loudness setting, or [Auphonic](https://auphonic.com) (its free tier gives about 2 hrs a month).

## 6. Hosting and distribution

**How it works:** you upload episodes to a **host**, and the host generates an **RSS feed**. You submit that feed **once** to each directory: Apple, Spotify, YouTube, Amazon, Pocket Casts and others. After that, new episodes show up everywhere automatically.

### Hosts

| Host | Price | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Spotify for Creators** | Free, unlimited | Native video podcasts and auto-distribution to Spotify. Its Partner Program needs 1,000 engaged listeners, 2,000 hours and 3 episodes ([The Podcast Consultant](https://thepodcastconsultant.com/blog/spotify-for-creators)) |
| **RSS.com** | Free tier; paid about $5–12/mo | A usable free tier, unlimited storage on paid plans ([Dupple](https://dupple.com/learn/best-podcast-hosting)) |
| **Buzzsprout** | Free tier (episodes expire after 90 days); paid about $12–15/mo | Friendliest for beginners ([Dupple](https://dupple.com/learn/best-podcast-hosting), [CostBench](https://costbench.com/stats/podcast-hosting-pricing-2026/)) |
| **Captivate** | From about $17/mo | Growth tools and a website builder |
| **Transistor** | From about $19/mo | Unlimited shows; good if you'll run more than one podcast |

**Recommendation:** start on **Spotify for Creators** (free, handles video) or **RSS.com's free tier**. Any reputable host lets you move your RSS feed later without losing subscribers.

### Getting listed

- **Apple Podcasts:** sign in to [Apple Podcasts Connect](https://podcastsconnect.apple.com) with your Apple ID, add the show by RSS URL, and wait for review (usually from a few hours to a few days).
- **Spotify:** this is automatic if you host on Spotify for Creators. Otherwise, submit your RSS feed through Spotify for Creators.
- **YouTube:** go to YouTube Studio, then Create, then **"Submit RSS feed."** Verify with the code emailed to the address in your feed. YouTube creates episode videos (a static cover image if you publish audio only), and they start out private for you to review ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2662754), [Soundstripe](https://www.soundstripe.com/blogs/how-to-start-a-podcast-on-youtube)). For a **video** podcast, upload the real videos directly and add them to a playlist marked as a podcast.
- Also submit to Amazon Music/Audible, Pocket Casts and Overcast. Most hosts do this in one click.

### Cover art specs (Apple)

- **Square, 3000 × 3000 px** (1400 px minimum), **JPG or PNG, RGB, no transparency**, and ideally **500 KB or smaller** ([Apple artwork requirements](https://podcasters.apple.com/support/artwork-requirements), [Transistor](https://support.transistor.fm/en/article/specs-for-podcast-cover-art-on-apple-podcasts-spotify-etc-1dyjud8)).
- Keep it to the show name and key art, in big text that's still readable as a thumbnail. Apple offers a [template](https://podcasters.apple.com/support/5514-show-cover-template).
- You can make it in Adobe Express, Photoshop (free through Harvard) or Canva.

## 7. Music and legal

### Royalty-free music

| Source | Cost | Licensing notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Pixabay Music** | Free | Commercial use OK, no attribution required ([ToolRadar](https://toolradar.com/tools/pixabay-music)) |
| **YouTube Audio Library** | Free (needs a YouTube channel) | Some tracks require attribution; check each one ([Foxi Music](https://www.foximusic.com/blog/best-royalty-free-music-sites-for-creators/)) |
| **Free Music Archive** | Free | Creative Commons. Watch out for NC (non-commercial) and BY (attribution) terms |
| **Epidemic Sound** | About $6–18/mo | Clear podcast licensing; tracks stay cleared for content published while you subscribed |
| **Artlist / Soundstripe** | About $10–17/mo | Same idea as Epidemic Sound |

### Copyright basics (not legal advice)

- **Don't use popular songs.** That includes intros and short clips. "Under 30 seconds" is a myth, and Spotify and YouTube will flag the episode.
- **Fair use** (commentary, criticism, news) is a defense decided case by case, not a rule. Keep any clips short and actually comment on them.
- Your own show name and artwork are yours. Check that the name isn't already taken on Apple and Spotify before you launch.

### Consent and guest releases

- **Massachusetts is an all-party-consent state.** Secretly recording a conversation is a crime there. Say "we're recording" on tape at the start of every interview ([Romano Law](https://www.romanolaw.com/can-i-record-a-conversation-in-massachusetts/)).
- **Use a short guest release form** (a Google Form or e-signature works). It should cover: guest name and contact; permission to record audio and video; the right to edit; the right to publish and distribute on any platform; use of name, voice and likeness for promotion and social clips; transcripts and AI summaries; and no payment unless agreed otherwise ([Matchmaker.fm](https://amplify.matchmaker.fm/podcast-guest-release-form/), [Content Allies](https://contentallies.com/learn/podcast-guest-release)).
- For student guests, consider offering a review window or a pre-agreed list of off-limits topics. It builds trust.

## 8. Recommended end-to-end beginner workflow

1. **Plan.** Write a one-page show concept, an episode outline and 3–5 guest questions. Name the show and check the name is free on Apple and Spotify.
2. **Set up (one-time).** Install Adobe Creative Cloud through HUIT. Make the cover art (3000×3000). Create a Spotify for Creators (or RSS.com) account. Set up a guest release form.
3. **Prepare the room.** Use the closet, blankets or Cabot studio. Put the mic a fist's distance from your mouth, wear headphones, and run Adobe Podcast Mic Check.
4. **Record.**
   - Solo or in person: GarageBand (or the Cabot studio), 48 kHz WAV, one track per mic.
   - Remote: Riverside (or Zencastr), with separate tracks, guests on headphones, and a recorded "we're recording" consent at the start.
   - Record 10 seconds of room silence at the start (useful for noise reduction).
5. **Back up.** Copy the raw files to iCloud or Google Drive right away.
6. **Clean.** If the room was noisy, run the audio through Adobe Podcast Enhance (or Descript Studio Sound).
7. **Edit.** In Descript (transcript-based) or GarageBand/Audition: cut mistakes and long pauses, then add the intro and outro music from Pixabay or Epidemic Sound. For video, use Descript, Premiere or DaVinci Resolve.
8. **Master.** Normalize to -16 LUFS stereo (-19 for mono), with a true peak of -1 dBTP or lower. Export a 128 kbps MP3 (plus a 1080p MP4 if you're doing video).
9. **Write the metadata.** Generate a transcript and draft show notes with AI, then fact-check them. Write a title, a 2–3 sentence description, guest links and chapter markers.
10. **Publish.** Upload to your host and schedule the episode.
11. **Distribute (once).** Submit the RSS feed to Apple Podcasts Connect, YouTube Studio ("Submit RSS feed"), Amazon and others. Spotify is automatic on Spotify for Creators.
12. **Promote.** Cut 2–3 vertical clips (Riverside Magic Clips or Descript) for Instagram, TikTok and Shorts. Share in Harvard House lists and club channels.
13. **Repeat on a schedule.** Consistency (for example every other week) matters more than polish. Launch with 2–3 episodes at once.
