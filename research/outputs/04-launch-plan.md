# Podcast Launch Toolkit

*For Carolyn: first-time podcaster, Harvard student, topic still open. Sources are linked inline and listed at the end.*

**How to use this:** Start with the Concept Worksheet (Section 1). Everything else depends on the answers, so give it one focused hour before you name anything or buy anything. After that, follow the 4-week timeline (Section 3), using the templates as you go.

---

## 1. Concept Worksheet

Answer each question in a sentence or two. If an answer is vague, the show will be too. Revisit the worksheet after recording your first episode.

### A. Topic
1. **What could I talk about for 50 episodes without running out of ideas?**
   *Guidance:* Write down 20 possible episode titles. If you stall at 8, the topic is too narrow, or you don't care about it enough.
2. **What do I know, or have access to, that most people don't?** (Classes, labs, clubs, professors, a hometown, a job, a hobby.)
   *Guidance:* Your access is your edge. At Harvard that could mean guests (faculty, visiting speakers, classmates with unusual stories) that other shows can't easily book.
3. **Why me, why now?**
   *Guidance:* One sentence. "I'm a student living through X, and I'm going to talk to the people figuring it out" is enough.

### B. Audience
4. **Who is the one listener I'm making this for?** Describe one specific person: age, what they're worried about, where they listen (walking to class, at the gym, on YouTube while studying).
   *Guidance:* Recent growth advice keeps coming back to being hyper-specific about your audience ([Podfluence](https://podfluence.beehiiv.com/p/podcasting-in-2025-most-shows-fail-here-s-how-to-beat-the-odds)). "College students" is too broad. "First-gen students in their first year at a big research university" is specific enough to work with.
5. **What should that listener feel, know, or do after an episode?**
   *Guidance:* Choose one main outcome: learn something, feel less alone, laugh, or decide something.
6. **Where does this listener already spend time?** Name subreddits, Instagram accounts, newsletters, campus groups, and other podcasts.
   *Guidance:* This list turns into your promotion plan (Section 7).

### C. Angle
7. **What existing shows cover this topic, and how will mine be different?** Find 3 to 5 of them on Apple Podcasts or Spotify.
   *Guidance:* Fill in the blank: "It's like ___, but ___." For example: "It's like Hidden Brain, but every guest is an undergrad."
8. **What's my point of view?** Am I a curious outsider, an insider explaining, a skeptic, or a friend thinking out loud?
9. **The one-sentence pitch:** "[Show name] is a [format] podcast for [audience] about [topic], where [what makes it different]."

### D. Name
10. **Three to five name candidates** (see Section 2 before deciding).
11. **Can someone tell from the name alone what the show is about?** If not, does a subtitle or description make it clear?

### E. Format
12. **Solo, co-host, or interview?**
    *Guidance:* Interviews are easiest for beginners because the guest brings the content. Solo is the hardest to keep up. With a co-host it's more fun, but you have to coordinate two schedules.
13. **Length?** *Guidance:* Make episodes as long as they need to be and no longer. For a student show, 20 to 35 minutes is realistic to edit and fits a walk or commute.
14. **Release frequency?** *Guidance:* Pick a schedule you can keep through midterms and finals. Every other week, done consistently, is better than weekly for a month followed by silence. Plan seasons (for example, 8 episodes per semester) so you have built-in breaks.
15. **Audio-only or video too?** *Guidance:* YouTube is now the most-used platform for podcast listening in the US. 33% of weekly podcast listeners say they use it most ([Edison Research, Infinite Dial 2025](https://podnews.net/press-release/edison-research-infinite-dial-2025)). Even a simple webcam or phone video gives you material for clips. Audio-only is fine if video would stop you from launching.
16. **What recurring segment or ritual makes the show recognizable?** (A signature closing question, a "one thing I learned this week" segment, a listener question.)

## 2. Show Naming

### Tips
- **Keep it short.** Aim for under four words, roughly 15 to 20 characters ([Libsyn](https://libsyn.com/blog/?p=14200)). It needs to be readable on a thumbnail.
- **Clear beats clever.** A name that hints at the topic helps people find you in search. You can add one or two relevant keywords, but don't stuff the title ([Libsyn](https://libsyn.com/blog/?p=14200)).
- **Don't game the title.** Apple can reject or remove a show whose title or author field is mostly a list of keywords. Put keywords in your **description** instead ([Libsyn](https://libsyn.com/blog/?p=14200); [Podcasts keywords guide](https://podstatus.com/blog/podcast-keywords-guide)).
- **No trademarks.** Leave out brand names, and that includes "Harvard" unless you've confirmed university branding rules allow it. A student org name could also be a problem.
- **Say it out loud.** If a friend hears it once, can they spell it into a search bar? Avoid odd spellings, numbers, and puns that only work in print.
- **Make it fit future episodes.** "Freshman Year Diaries" stops making sense when you're a junior.
- **Test it.** Text 5 people the name plus your one-sentence pitch and ask what they expect the show to be. Background reading: [Pacific Content on naming](https://pacific-content.com/podcast-titles/).

### Availability Checklist (about 15 minutes per name)
- [ ] **Apple Podcasts:** search the exact name and close variants.
- [ ] **Spotify:** search in the app.
- [ ] **YouTube:** search the name, then check whether `youtube.com/@yourname` is available.
- [ ] **Podcast Index** (podcastindex.org) or **Listen Notes** (listennotes.com): these cover nearly every RSS feed, including inactive shows.
- [ ] **Instagram and TikTok handle:** try `@showname` first, then `@shownamepod` or `@shownamepodcast` as fallbacks. Use the same handle on every platform.
- [ ] **X / Threads / Bluesky handle:** optional, depending on where your audience is.
- [ ] **Domain:** check `.com` (or `.fm` / `.show` / `.co`) at any registrar. You don't have to buy it yet, but confirm it isn't taken by something embarrassing.
- [ ] **Google:** search the name in quotes. Check whether a company, product, or controversy already owns those search results.
- [ ] **Trademark (quick check):** search USPTO's free trademark search for the exact phrase.
- [ ] **Campus:** make sure no existing student org or publication uses a similar name.

If a name clears every item, **claim the handles the same day**, even before launch.

## 3. Four-Week Pre-Launch Timeline

*Assumes the Concept Worksheet is done. Plan for 4 to 6 hours a week. Launch day is the end of Week 4.*

### Week 1: Decide and Set Up
- [ ] Finalize the concept: pitch, audience, format, schedule.
- [ ] Pick the name, run the availability checklist, and claim the social handles.
- [ ] Choose a hosting platform. Free or cheap options include Spotify for Creators, Buzzsprout's free tier, and RSS.com. The host creates the RSS feed that every app reads.
- [ ] Gather gear. A USB dynamic mic (about $50 to $100) beats a laptop mic. Check whether Harvard's libraries or media labs lend equipment or rent recording rooms. Software: Audacity or GarageBand (free), Descript or Riverside (free tiers) for remote interviews and editing.
- [ ] List 10 to 15 episode ideas and 8 to 10 possible guests.
- [ ] Record a 5-minute **test episode** that will never be published. Listen back and fix your room, mic distance, and levels.

### Week 2: Record the Launch Batch
- [ ] Email or DM guests for Episodes 1 to 3. Offer 2 or 3 time slots and send prep notes (see Section 4).
- [ ] Write episode plans with the template in Section 4.
- [ ] Record **Episode 1** and the **trailer** (60 to 90 seconds on what the show is, who it's for, and when new episodes come out ([Vida launch checklist](https://vida.io/blog/podcast-launch-checklist))).
- [ ] Order or design cover art: square, 3000 x 3000 px, JPEG or PNG, RGB, no transparency. Include only the show name plus key art, and keep important elements out of the bottom fifth ([Apple artwork requirements](https://podcasters.apple.com/support/artwork-requirements)). Canva's free templates are enough. Check legibility at thumbnail size.
- [ ] Choose free intro and outro music with a clear license (YouTube Audio Library, Pixabay Music, or a musician friend with written permission).

### Week 3: Record, Edit, Submit
- [ ] Record **Episodes 2 and 3**.
- [ ] Edit all three plus the trailer. Remove dead air and false starts, normalize loudness (about -16 LUFS for stereo is the usual target), and add intro and outro.
- [ ] Write show notes for each episode (Section 5).
- [ ] Upload the **trailer only** to your host and publish it so the RSS feed exists.
- [ ] Submit the RSS feed to **Apple Podcasts Connect**, **Spotify**, **YouTube** (via YouTube Studio RSS ingestion or manual upload), and Amazon Music. Approval can take a few days, which is why you submit now.
- [ ] Write the show description: two or three sentences, keyword-friendly, and specific about the audience.

### Week 4: Warm Up and Launch
- [ ] Build a **launch list** of 30 to 50 friends, classmates, club members, and family who agree to listen and follow on day one. A warm group that commits before public promotion is one of the four pillars of a strong launch ([Podder](https://www.podderapp.com/post/launch-strategy-for-podcasters)).
- [ ] Prepare promo assets: 3 to 6 vertical clips (30 to 60 seconds, with captions), 1 audiogram or quote graphic per episode, and a launch post.
- [ ] Listen to every episode on at least two devices or apps to catch errors ([Vida](https://vida.io/blog/podcast-launch-checklist)).
- [ ] Confirm the show appears in Apple, Spotify, and YouTube search.
- [ ] Schedule Episodes 1 to 3 to publish **together** on launch day (early morning).
- [ ] **Launch day:** post everywhere, message your launch list personally (not a mass blast), and ask for one specific action: "Follow and listen to Episode 1."
- [ ] Have Episode 4 planned, and ideally recorded, before launch day so you don't immediately fall behind.

## 4. Episode Planning Template

Copy this for every episode.

```
EPISODE # ___   Working title: ______________________________
Planned release date: ________   Target length: ___ min
Format: [ ] solo  [ ] interview  [ ] co-host  [ ] other: ______

GOAL
  After this episode, the listener will ______________________ (know / feel / do).

ONE-SENTENCE HOOK (first 30 seconds)
  "______________________________________________________"

GUEST (if any)
  Name / role: ______________________   Contact: ______________
  Why them: _______________________________________________
  Intro line I'll read: ____________________________________
  Pre-interview done? [ ] yes   Release form signed? [ ] yes
  Bio, headshot, and links collected? [ ] yes

OUTLINE
  0:00  Cold open / hook clip: _____________________________
  0:30  Intro + what this episode is about: _________________
  ___   Segment 1: _______________________________________
          Key questions / points: ___________________________
  ___   Segment 2: _______________________________________
          Key questions / points: ___________________________
  ___   Segment 3: _______________________________________
  ___   Recurring segment / signature question: ____________
  ___   Wrap-up: the one takeaway is _______________________
  ___   Call to action (ONE): _____________________________
  ___   Tease next episode: _______________________________

MUST-ASK QUESTIONS (top 3)
  1. ___________________  2. ___________________  3. ___________________

BACKUP QUESTIONS (if things stall)
  - ___________________  - ___________________

CLIP-WORTHY MOMENTS TO AIM FOR
  - (a story, a strong opinion, a surprising fact) _____________

RESEARCH / LINKS TO MENTION
  - ________________________________________________

POST-PRODUCTION
  [ ] edited  [ ] show notes  [ ] clips pulled (___)  [ ] uploaded
  [ ] scheduled  [ ] guest sent links + clips to share
```

**Guest prep note to send ahead (short):** the date, time, and link; how long it will take; 3 or 4 topics you'll cover (not exact questions); a request to use headphones and sit in a quiet room; when the episode will come out.

## 5. Show-Notes Template

Show notes help with search and give listeners somewhere to find the links you mention. Include timestamps for each launch episode ([Vida](https://vida.io/blog/podcast-launch-checklist)).

```
TITLE: [Clear, specific, searchable. Guest name and topic if relevant]
  e.g. "Why Freshman Year Feels Lonely (and What Helps) with Dr. Jane Doe"

FIRST 1–2 SENTENCES (these show up in app previews, so make them count):
  In this episode, [who] and I talk about [topic], including [hook].

IN THIS EPISODE
  - [Key point / question 1]
  - [Key point / question 2]
  - [Key point / question 3]

CHAPTERS / TIMESTAMPS
  00:00  Intro
  02:15  [Topic]
  10:40  [Topic]
  24:30  [Signature question]
  28:00  Wrap-up

ABOUT THE GUEST
  [2–3 sentence bio]. Find them at: [link] | [handle]

LINKS & RESOURCES MENTIONED
  - [Resource] – [URL]

CONNECT WITH THE SHOW
  Follow on [Apple] | [Spotify] | [YouTube]
  Instagram/TikTok: @______   Email/newsletter: ______
  Got a question or story for a future episode? [form link]

CREDITS
  Hosted and produced by Carolyn Dorantes. Music: [track] by [artist], [license].
```

## 6. The First Three Episodes

### What Each Should Accomplish
| Episode | Job | What it should contain |
|---|---|---|
| **Trailer** (60–90 s) | Get a follow before anyone has heard a full episode | Who you are, who the show is for, what they'll get, and when episodes come out. End with "Follow so you don't miss Episode 1." |
| **Episode 1: The Promise** | Show the experience in its best form | Your **strongest** episode, not an "about me" episode. Keep the intro brief (2 minutes max on why you're making this), then go straight into real content. A new listener should know within 30 seconds what the show is like. |
| **Episode 2: The Range** | Show the format holds up across different subjects | A different guest or subtopic in the same structure. It proves Episode 1 wasn't a one-off and shows how wide the show can go. |
| **Episode 3: The Depth / Connection** | Get people coming back | Something more personal, a deeper dive, or the episode that most clearly serves your one specific listener. Introduce the listener call to action here (submit questions, join the newsletter), so you'll have material for later episodes. |

Rules that apply to all three: make **Episode 1 your strongest content** even if you recorded it last. Make each episode understandable on its own, because some people will start with Episode 3. End each episode by pointing to the next one.

### Why Launch With 2 or 3 Episodes at Once
- **Bingeing leads to following.** A new listener who likes Episode 1 can go straight to the next episode, and that second listen is when people decide to follow ([The Audacity to Podcast](https://theaudacitytopodcast.com/8-reasons-to-launch-your-podcast-with-a-few-episodes/); [Castmagic](https://www.castmagic.io/blog/launching-a-successful-podcast-12-strategies-to-grow-your-audience)).
- **It shows what the show is.** One episode could be a one-off. Three show a pattern, so people understand the format and what to expect ([Podcasting for Business Owners](https://podcastingforbusinessowners.buzzsprout.com/2391340/episodes/17141777-44-what-to-put-in-your-first-3-podcast-episodes)).
- **It signals that the show is active**, which helps when apps decide what to recommend ([Castmagic](https://www.castmagic.io/blog/launching-a-successful-podcast-12-strategies-to-grow-your-audience)).
- **It gives you a buffer.** Your next deadline is two weeks after launch, not two days. That matters during a semester.
- **It concentrates your launch.** You only get one launch day of attention, and three episodes give that attention more to land on.
- **A caveat:** this is a best practice, not a rule. Apple does not require three episodes to launch or to be featured ([Podder](https://www.podderapp.com/post/launch-strategy-for-podcasters)). If you only have two episodes that are truly good, launch with two rather than add a weak third. Some marketing sites cite exact percentage increases for multi-episode launches. Treat those as anecdotal.

## 7. Promotion on a Student Budget

### Social Clips (Your Main Engine)
- **Pull 3 to 6 clips per episode.** A 30-minute episode usually has one strong moment every 5 minutes or so ([Choppity](https://www.choppity.com/blog/how-to-grow-a-podcast-on-youtube)).
- **Format:** vertical 9:16, 30 to 60 seconds, **burned-in captions** (many people watch with the sound off), and a hook in the **first 2 seconds**. Start mid-thought with the most interesting line, not with "So today we're talking about..."
- **Post to** Instagram Reels, TikTok, and YouTube Shorts. Shorts link most directly to the full episode on YouTube ([Choppity](https://www.choppity.com/blog/how-to-grow-a-podcast-on-youtube)). About 62% of company podcast teams post clips at least weekly ([Riverside](https://marketing.riverside.com/blog/how-companies-are-growing-their-video-podcasts-audience)), so posting regularly is the norm.
- **Free tools:** CapCut (editing and auto-captions), Descript free tier, Canva (quote graphics), and the free tiers of auto-clipping tools such as Opus Clip.
- **YouTube basics:** a clear thumbnail, chapters in the description, and captions. Chapters and captions are linked to view increases ([Podfluence](https://podfluence.beehiiv.com/p/podcasting-in-2025-most-shows-fail-here-s-how-to-beat-the-odds)).
- **Cadence:** about 3 clips a week is manageable. Batch-edit them in one sitting.

### Campus Channels (Your Unfair Advantage)
- **House and dorm email lists, and department or concentration newsletters.** Ask the list moderator or administrator before posting.
- **Student publications:** pitch The Crimson or other student publications on a story about the show, or offer a guest column tied to an episode.
- **Student radio** (WHRB): ask about a cross-promo spot or guest appearance.
- **Clubs related to your topic:** have members as guests. They'll share the episode with their own club.
- **Faculty guests:** professors often share episodes with their classes or departments. Ask politely.
- **Physical:** QR-code flyers in houses, the Smith Campus Center, and libraries (follow posting rules).
- **Class group chats and GroupMe:** share an episode only when it's actually relevant to that group.

### Newsletters
- **Start your own on day one** with a free Substack, Beehiiv, or Buttondown account. Send a short note with each episode: one takeaway, one link, one question for readers. An email list is the one channel you own outright.
- **Pitch other newsletters** in your topic area for a mention. Offer something useful (a quote, a resource) rather than just a request to promote you.

### Cross-Promotion
- **Guest swaps:** appear on other student podcasts (at Harvard and at other schools) and invite those hosts onto yours.
- **Promo swaps:** trade 30-second trailers that each of you plays in your own episodes with a show of similar size and a similar audience.
- **Make sharing easy for guests:** send each guest a ready-made clip, a quote graphic, and a suggested caption on release day. Guests are your best distribution, but only if sharing takes them almost no effort.
- **Communities:** answer questions in relevant subreddits and Discords. Link to an episode only when it actually answers the question.

### Simple Metrics to Watch (Monthly Is Enough)
| Metric | Where | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Downloads per episode at 7 and 30 days** | Your host | The basic growth number. Compare episodes against each other, not against big shows. |
| **Followers** | Apple Podcasts Connect, Spotify for Creators | Shows whether people are committing to the show. |
| **Average consumption / retention** | Apple, Spotify, YouTube Studio | Shows **where people drop off**. A drop in the first minute means the intro needs work. |
| **Clip views → profile visits** | Instagram, TikTok, YouTube analytics | Shows which kinds of clips bring people over to the full episode. |
| **Newsletter subscribers** | Your newsletter tool | Your most loyal audience. |
| **Listener replies** | Comments, DMs, email, question form | Qualitative signal for which topics land. |

**Benchmark for perspective:** most independent shows get modest numbers early on. Judge yourself against **your own trend over a semester**, not against celebrity shows. Pick **one thing to improve per month** (the hook, the thumbnails, clip style) rather than change everything at once.

## 8. Recording Day Checklist (One Page)

**The night before**
- [ ] Episode plan finalized, with must-ask questions marked
- [ ] Guest confirmed (time, time zone, link) and reminder sent
- [ ] Devices charged; laptop updated (or updates postponed)
- [ ] Enough free storage (at least 10 GB)

**60 minutes before**
- [ ] Room set up: soft surfaces, curtains closed, door shut, "Recording" sign posted
- [ ] Fans, AC, and the mini fridge off if possible; phone on Do Not Disturb (guest too)
- [ ] Notifications off on the computer; unneeded apps closed
- [ ] Water nearby (no ice, since it clinks; no carbonated drinks)

**Gear check (15 minutes before)**
- [ ] Mic connected and **selected as the input** in the recording software (the most common mistake)
- [ ] Headphones on, which prevents echo and feedback
- [ ] Mouth about a fist's width from the mic, slightly off-axis
- [ ] Levels: peaks around -12 to -6 dB, never touching 0 (red)
- [ ] 30-second test recording, played back **through headphones**
- [ ] Video (if used): camera at eye level, light in front of you, clean background, framing checked
- [ ] Backup recorder running (phone voice memo nearby)

**With a guest (remote)**
- [ ] Guest using headphones and a wired or close-to-router connection
- [ ] Separate tracks per person on (Riverside, Zencastr, Zoom "separate audio files")
- [ ] Confirm the guest consents to recording and publishing; note how they want their name and title stated
- [ ] Ask the guest to clap once at the start (a sync point for editing)

**While recording**
- [ ] Record 10 seconds of **room silence** (used for noise reduction in editing)
- [ ] Say the date and episode number at the start (the "slate")
- [ ] If you stumble, pause, then repeat the whole sentence (easy to cut later)
- [ ] Mark good moments: note the timestamp for clips, or clap once
- [ ] Check the clock at the halfway point; cover the must-ask questions first
- [ ] Ask your signature question and record a clean intro and outro

**Right after**
- [ ] Stop the recording and **save immediately**; wait for remote uploads to finish (100%)
- [ ] Back up files to two places (laptop plus cloud) before closing anything
- [ ] Thank the guest; collect bio, links, headshot, and handles; tell them the release date
- [ ] Write 3 notes: best clip moments, anything to cut, ideas for a follow-up episode
- [ ] Name files consistently: `EP03_guestname_YYYY-MM-DD_raw.wav`
